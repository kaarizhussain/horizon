An AI bias checker that helps companies write job descriptions for the best candidate — not the most credentialed one.

The problem

Most job descriptions are written to impress internally, not to help real people understand the role. Inflated requirements and exclusionary language push qualified candidates to self-select out — companies shrink their talent pool before the first application arrives.

A real example

A "Junior Project Coordinator" role asked for 3–5 years' experience, a PMP certification, and advanced Excel — for a job that was really scheduling meetings, tracking tasks, and updating status reports. A strong candidate read it, assumed they weren't qualified, and never applied. The company never knew why.

The MVP — five features

  • AI Rewriter — rewrites the posting in clear, plain language a wider audience can read.
  • Requirement Splitter — forces every requirement into must-have vs. nice-to-have, so teams see when they're over-filtering talent.
  • Bias Detector — flags gender-coded, elitist, and culture-fit language, with plain reasons and suggested alternatives.
  • Transparency Prompt — catches missing pay, schedule, or logistics before the post goes live.
  • Fairness Score — a single 0–100 signal across language, requirements, transparency, and readability.

How I'd measure success

  • +20-point average Fairness Score lift, edited vs. original
  • Over 60% of bias-flag suggestions accepted by recruiters
  • 2+ unnecessary requirements removed per posting

The risk I'd lose sleep over

That FairPost becomes a compliance shield — a high score that lets companies look fair on paper while biased hiring continues behind it. My guardrail: track downstream hiring outcomes, not just posting quality, and make that data visible to DEI leaders rather than hiding it behind a score.

What I learned

An AI product's success has less to do with model quality than with whether it changes human behavior. The real challenge isn't intelligence — it's placement in the workflow, incentives, and enforcement.

Booking a kids' party in under a minute — turning an afternoon of phone calls and unclear quotes into a few taps.

Product Designer · UX · UI · Prototype · iOS, mobile-first · end-to-end concept

The problem

Parents booking a birthday for under-fives hit opaque pricing, confusing policies, and a string of back-and-forth calls just to lock in a date. It's stressful for families — and a constant interruption for the play-space staff fielding the calls.

The goal

Make booking transparent and self-serve, so families book in minutes and staff stop answering the phone.

The solution — a guided flow where price is never a surprise

  • Two clear packages with the full price shown up front — one decision, no ambiguity
  • Extras as simple toggles, with the price sitting right next to each switch
  • One guest stepper — I folded a confusing "additional kids" add-on and a separate guest count into a single control (10 included, $15 each after, capped at 30)
  • An itemized summary with a $100 deposit to hold the date — full payment isn't due until the party

Key decisions

  • Show the running total, always — it removes the "how much will this actually be?" anxiety that drives the phone calls
  • Deposit, not full payment — lowers commitment friction for parents while protecting the venue's calendar
  • Policies in context — socks, waivers, and arrival windows appear at summary and confirmation, not buried in an FAQ

What I learned

Defining MVP scope is easiest when it's anchored to a concrete business need, and user empathy is the fastest way to prioritize what ships first.
